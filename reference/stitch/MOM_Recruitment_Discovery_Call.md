**MINUTES OF MEETING (MOM)**

*Discovery / Introductory Call — Recruitment Process Outsourcing Proposal*

# 1. Meeting Details
| Meeting Type | Introductory / Discovery Call |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Vendor company to understand client's recruitment workflow and propose value-added services (lead generation, candidate sourcing, AI automation) |
| Participants | Vendor Representative (Agency — Web Dev, Digital Marketing & AI Automation Services); Client — "Dave" (Owner, Retail Recruitment & Executive Placement Consultancy) |
| Client Industry | Recruitment / Staffing — Retail, C-Store & Food Service sector |
| Outcome | Client conditionally open to proceeding; requested a free demo / proof-of-concept before commercial discussion |

# 2. Company Background (as presented by Vendor)
Seven years operating; UK, Europe, Germany, US, MENA/North Africa. Web dev, digital marketing, AI automation.

# 3. Client Business Overview
## 3.1 Business Model
Recruitment consultancy + executive placement. Verticals: retail, C-store, food service. Two-sided model (candidate pipeline + client acquisition). Two-person operation (client + one recruiter).

## 3.2 Current Workflow & Tools
"TA Tracker" proprietary pipeline database. Sources from internal DB + LinkedIn. Scoring algorithm rates JD fit as %. 90%+ closer review, 95%+ prioritized, 97–98%+ typically converts to offer. Shortlists top 2–3 only. Apollo for contact enrichment (~83% hit rate cited). Outreach via LinkedIn, phone/text, email in parallel. Mostly on-site, some hybrid/remote.

# 4. Vendor's Proposed Value Proposition
Paid/organic lead gen (Meta/Instagram); dedicated outreach team; broader geographic sourcing (Caribbean, North Africa, Europe); automated outreach/scheduling; curated shortlists only; client's own platform accounts used per-client.

# 5. Client's Key Requirements & Concerns
Quality over quantity non-negotiable — stated as a "failure" if volume/speed lacks quality. Speed valuable only secondarily. Poor submissions risk the client's own end-client relationships. Account-ownership question raised, unanswered. Pricing deferred, relationship/results-based model proposed, no cost until concept proven.

# 6. Agreed Next Steps
POC structure agreed before commercial commitment: free demo (tentatively "next week"); separate technical-team call; client shares 2 JDs once demo scheduled; parallel independent sourcing benchmark; compared on speed + quality against client's 90%/95%/97–98% standard; vendor proceeds to paid engagement only if faster AND equal-or-better quality.

# 7. Action Items
| # | Action Item | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Schedule technical team call | Vendor | Pending |
| 2 | Prepare/deliver free demo | Vendor | Pending |
| 3 | Confirm outreach account model | Vendor | Open |
| 4 | Select two roles, share JDs | Client (Dave) | Pending |
| 5 | Run parallel candidate search | Client (Dave) | Alongside demo |
| 6 | Compare results, decide | Both | After benchmark |
| 7 | Provide pricing proposal | Vendor | Deferred |

# 8. Risks & Open Points
No pricing defined. Success criteria subjective ("equal or better") — needs quantifying. No demo date fixed. Account-access model unresolved. Client has low tolerance for underperformance — one shot to prove value.

# 9. Analysis & Recommendations
## 9.1 Reading of the Client
Experienced, quality-driven operator (former retail CEO), lean process, proprietary scoring. Protective of his own client reputation. Repeated quality-over-quantity at least seven times — treat as the primary success metric.

## 9.2 Recommendations for the Vendor
Define objective numeric success criteria before the demo. Lock a firm demo date quickly. Prepare a written account/platform-usage summary. Position the demo around matching/beating the 90%/95% benchmarks, not raw speed. Prepare a draft pricing framework in advance.
